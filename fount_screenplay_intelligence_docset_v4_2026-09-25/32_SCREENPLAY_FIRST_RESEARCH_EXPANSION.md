# Screenplay-first research expansion

Research date: 2026-09-26. This extends `SOURCES.md`; it does not discard the earlier research. Sources provide inspirations and bounded evidence, not proof that Fount improves a screenplay. Requirements are our synthesis; no cited practitioner endorses Fount.

## The change in emphasis

Fount exists to help someone discover, write, hear, reconsider, and finish a screenplay they want to make. It is not primarily a screenplay-grading system. Analysis earns its place when it helps a writer make a decision, preserve something valuable, or try an alternative. Elixir is a chosen medium, not a reason to build distributed-system features.

Earlier v4 work covers reader experience, diagnosis, uncertainty, and notes unusually well. The missing counterweight is writing: a wanted image before a plot, a scene before an outline, voice before correctness, a revision before another report. Keep both directions available: pages can create the outline, and an outline can create pages.

## Primary-source research ledger

### R01 — Desire without compulsory conflict

[Céline Sciamma, BAFTA lecture, 2019](https://www.bafta.org/media-centre/press-releases/screenwriters-lecture-series-2019-celine-sciamma/) describes developing desired material, examining apparently necessary scenes, and seeking tension without making conflict the universal organizing principle.

Product inference: keep wanted images, situations, sounds, and moments separate from obligations. A scene may create intimacy, attention, rhythm, or recognition without a winner or rising stakes. Test an intentionally quiet encounter and require a leave-it-alone interpretation.

### R02 — Explore a scene before polishing it

[John August, How to write a scene, 2007](https://johnaugust.com/2007/write-scene) offers questions about purpose, omission, participants, location, and possible starts, followed by rough writing.

Product inference: compare different entrances and actions, then produce a rough candidate. Questions are optional aids, not a mandatory form. A writer can start with pages and refuse explanation.

### R03 — Discovery rather than imposed certainty

[Charlie Kaufman, BAFTA lecture, 2011](https://www.bafta.org/media-centre/press-releases/screenwriters-lecture-charlie-kaufman/) makes a case for personal expression and the uncertainty of discovery rather than a transferable success formula.

Product inference: an exploratory branch may contradict the brief on purpose. Mark that departure and let the writer revise the brief or reject the branch. Do not steer every unusual proposal back toward the average screenplay.

### R04 — Place, physical action, and the screen

[Kelly Reichardt, Sundance interview on Meek's Cutoff, 2011](https://www.sundance.org/blogs/qa-director-kelly-reichardt-on-making-meeks-cutoff-3/) discusses writing with a particular landscape and translating material into visual filmmaking.

Product inference: offer a visual-and-sound pass on perceptible action, space, physical tasks, silence, and offscreen events. Dialogue, voiceover, and poetic action description remain legitimate. Cinematic writing is not a blanket ban on interiority or language.

### R05 — Discovery through relationship and performance

[Mike Leigh, BAFTA lecture, 2014](https://www.bafta.org/stories/mike-leigh-screenwriters-lecture/) describes discovery involving character, relationship, structure, and performance.

Product inference: support reversible rehearsal exercises and alternative tactics, including changing who is present or what one character thinks the other knows. Agent role-play is invention, not evidence about actors or authentic lived experience. It never becomes canon automatically.

### R06 — Assistance beyond replacement prose

[Wordcraft Writers Workshop](https://wordcraft-writers-workshop.appspot.com/learn) reports an eight-week study with thirteen professional writers. Accounts include brainstorming value alongside difficulty preserving voice and avoiding unsuitable output. [The authors' paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.05030) provides the research context.

Product inference: evaluate usable ideas, control, and time spent rejecting suggestions separately from prose acceptance. Offer fragments and questions as well as drafts. Rejection is valid, not an invitation to generate indefinitely.

### R07 — Hierarchical generation is one workflow

[Dramatron's research repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/dramatron) and [paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14958) describe hierarchical co-writing and research with fifteen industry professionals. They do not establish autonomous feature-screenplay quality.

Product inference: support outline-first generation without requiring it. Compare scene-first discovery and revision of human pages. Trace premise to candidate, but allow deliberate departures.

### R08 — Fluency can coexist with narrowing diversity

[Doshi and Hauser, Generative artificial intelligence enhances creativity but reduces the diversity of novel content](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.00506) studies short stories, not feature screenplays. Its results motivate caution about collective similarity; they are not a measured Fount result.

Product inference: alternatives must differ in dramatic mechanism, not just wording. Evaluate diversity and voice independently of fluency. One model's approval of another's output is not sufficient creativity validation.

### R09 — Everyday affordances matter

[Final Draft Beat Board](https://kb.finaldraft.com/hc/en-us/articles/27608580196244-What-is-the-Beat-Board) documents free organization of material. [WriterDuet revision tracking](https://www.writerduet.com/article/109-view-and-track-revisions-edits) documents inspecting changes. [Scriptation note transfer](https://help.scriptation.com/en/article/how-do-i-transfer-my-notes-1khpcd8/) illustrates notes surviving changing drafts.

Product inference: capture fragments, reorder scene intentions, compare versions, and preserve note context. These are workflow baselines, not evidence of competitor inferiority. Reusing an old note without proving its new anchor is a review risk.

### R10 — Preserve the actual writing

[Fountain syntax](https://fountain.io/syntax/) distinguishes screenplay elements from sections, synopses, notes, and boneyard material, and includes dual dialogue.

Product inference: fidelity and a correct reader-visible projection are creative requirements. A private note or discarded scene must not leak into reader knowledge, exports, or provider context without explicit inclusion. Retain FDX/Fountain roundtrip guarantees.

## What changes

Documents 33–36 are normative additions: writer workflows, human/Jev/LLM collaboration, four-XML handoff, and product acceptance demonstrations. Older analytical and safety contracts still apply. When a heuristic conflicts with declared intent, expose the tradeoff rather than treating the heuristic as law.

The distinctive proposition is the combination: pursue an unusual intention, see what pages support, rehearse materially different choices, inspect consequences, and own the decision. This is an ambition to test, not a claim of world-leading performance already achieved.

## Limits and follow-up

Practitioner accounts are situated methods, not consensus or experiments. Co-writing studies are small or concern shorter forms. Product documentation proves an affordance, not improved writing. Source availability does not grant corpus or provider-export rights.

Retain the human-validation plan. Include outline-first, discovery, visually led, and distinctive-dialogue writers. Record disagreement and unsuccessful sessions. Broaden language, culture, disability, genre, and career-stage coverage before broad usefulness claims; a convenience sample is not representative.

Recheck official submission and AI-disclosure requirements when relevant. Fount records provenance and decisions; it does not certify eligibility, authorship, legal compliance, commercial success, or audience response.
