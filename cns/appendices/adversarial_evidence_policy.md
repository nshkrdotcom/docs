# Appendix — Adversarial Evidence Policy

## Definition

An adversarial evidence environment is any setting where relevant evidence may be selectively generated, controlled, disclosed, withheld, altered, destroyed, framed, delayed, or made inaccessible by actors with incentives affected by the claim outcome.

## Policy

GCTS must represent adversarial evidence conditions explicitly. It must not collapse controlled non-production into simple lack of evidence, and it must not convert suspicion of non-production into proof.

## Allowed in likely-truth ranking

- record-generation duty;
- expected observability;
- access-state classification;
- source control;
- institutional incentive features;
- competing benign and strategic missingness worlds;
- calibrated missingness likelihood.

## Forbidden as strict proof

- motive alone;
- missing record alone;
- source control alone;
- institutional role alone;
- LLM assertion that a record is likely withheld.

## Required distinctions

- absent evidence vs evidence of absence;
- inaccessible record vs non-existent record;
- withheld record vs unknown missingness;
- strict proof vs likely truth;
- probability vs confidence.

## Enforcement

Runtime reports must show:

- record dependencies;
- access states;
- competing missingness worlds;
- strict support mass;
- likely-truth posterior;
- confidence;
- evidence that would change the ranking.
